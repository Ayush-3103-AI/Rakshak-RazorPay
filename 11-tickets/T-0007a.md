# T-0007a — Cost redefinition and the oracle-dominance invariant

**Day:** Sat 29 Aug · **Risk: high — the primary metric is unreadable until this lands** · **Cuttable: no** · **Depends on (blocked by):** T-0017

## Why this ticket exists

`savings` is the project's primary metric and it is currently **negative on every row,
including both perfect-foresight oracles**. The knapsack oracle scores −0.678 against
"hold everything" at +0.573. A perfect-foresight ceiling beaten by a trivial policy is
not a ceiling, and every downstream number is being read against it.

`results/summary.md` reports the cause honestly: 13.4 INR of false-positive cost per
100 INR of fraud loss, against `07-math.md §5`'s stated 400–600. **The fix is not to
tune the ratio into range.** T-0017 has already established that the two quantities are
definitionally wrong, and this ticket implements those corrected definitions and then
reports whatever ratio falls out.

## Context to read

`07-math.md §5` **as amended by T-0017** — the redefinitions and the demoted cross-check.
`results/summary.md` "Cost-matrix sanity check" for the failing row. `config.py:30-46`
and `:174` for the current provisional constants.

## Build — `src/rakshak/decision/cost.py` and `config.py`

### Implement the corrected definitions

- **`V_m` = expected lifetime gross margin**, not one window's MDR. Composed from MDR ×
  expected monthly volume × expected remaining merchant lifetime, each an independently
  sourced primitive with a range, per T-0017.
- **`L_m` = realised fraud loss**, not gross bad-state volume. Bad-state volume × an
  explicit realisation rate covering chargebacks, scheme fees and write-off.
- Every constant carries its citation and its uncertainty range **in `config.py`**, which
  the file docstring already establishes as the single home for cost parameters.
- Constants with no external source stay marked `ASSUMPTION`, as `w_analyst` and
  `P(churn|hold)` already are. The distinction between sourced and assumed must be
  visible in the code, not averaged away.

### The oracle-dominance invariant

Add an assertion, run as a test and as a harness precondition:

> The perfect-foresight ceiling must weakly dominate **every** policy scored in the same
> run, including the trivial ones — pass-everything, hold-everything, random.

This single check would have caught the current defect at T-0005 rather than T-0006.
It is the executable form of "set the oracle": a ceiling you can test is a ceiling.

If it fails after the redefinitions, **the failure is the finding** — report it, do not
adjust constants until it passes.

### Report the cross-check, do not close it

Recompute the FP-cost-per-100-fraud-loss ratio under the corrected definitions and print
it in `results/summary.md` exactly as now, with the 400–600 line labelled a **cross-check**
rather than a gate. If the corrected definitions produce 90, or 1,200, that is the number
that ships, alongside a stated explanation of the divergence.

## Done when

- `savings` is readable: both oracles score above every model and above hold-everything
- The oracle-dominance invariant is a committed test, and it passes
- Unit test against hand-computed `c_fp` and `L_m` on a 2-merchant fixture (FR-016)
- `results/summary.md`'s cost-matrix row reports the new ratio and labels 400–600 a
  cross-check, with any divergence stated
- Every cost constant in `config.py` carries a citation or an explicit `ASSUMPTION` tag,
  plus a range
- Determinism holds

## What this ticket must NOT do

- **Do not select constants by the ratio they produce.** Source first, compute second,
  report third. Choosing a realisation rate because it lands the ratio near 500 is
  tuning a parameter until a check passes — the practice `T-0016` explicitly forbids for
  the generator, and it would be worse here because `savings` is the headline metric.
- Do not build the policy or the sweep. That is T-0007b.
