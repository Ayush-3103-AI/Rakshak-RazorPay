## DID

### 1. `decision/policy.py` — one cost matrix, four uses

`expected_costs(p, params)` is `07-math.md` §5's matrix marginalised over `P(bad)`:

```
E[PASS]   = p·L_m
E[REVIEW] = c_rev + p·p_miss·L_m
E[HOLD]   = p·rho·L_m + (1-p)·c_fp(m)
```

It is linear in `p`, so passing a 0/1 label vector returns the *realised* cost instead of the
expected one. That single function therefore serves BMR's argmin, the Bahnsen scoring, the
`min(all-PASS, all-HOLD)` denominator and the perfect-hindsight ceiling. No second
implementation of any of those.

`bmr_action(posterior, params) -> (action, expected_cost_per_action)` is FR-015 and FR-016:
exactly one of `{PASS, REVIEW, HOLD}` plus the cost of every alternative, argmin under the cost
matrix, ties broken to the lower action code so it is deterministic.

### 2. FR-017 — capacity, with the binding constraint reported

`apply_capacity` solves BMR unconstrained first, then, if the implied review hours exceed `B`,
downgrades the **lowest-regret** REVIEWs to their own cheapest alternative — regret being
`min(E[PASS], E[HOLD]) − E[REVIEW]`. Every review costs the same `tau` (07-math.md §7's unit
review cost), so dropping smallest-regret-first is exactly optimal, and it is the same sort the
knapsack oracle does, on regret instead of on hindsight loss.

`PolicyResult` carries `binding_constraint` (`"capacity"` / `"none"`), `n_downgraded` and
`unconstrained_n_reviewed`. FR-017 says the binding constraint must be *reported*; a run where
the budget did nothing and a run where it forced 11 downgrades must not look alike from the
outside. Both `summary.md` and `sensitivity.md` print it **per model**, because the answer
differs by model at the same asymmetry.

### 3. The sweep range is derived, and here is the derivation

Six primitives move the FP-per-₹100 asymmetry and no others do:

* numerator, `c_fp = P(churn|hold)·g·v_m·ℓ_m + c_support` → `P_CHURN_GIVEN_HOLD`,
  `GROSS_MARGIN_RATE`, `MERCHANT_LIFETIME_MONTHS`, `COST_SUPPORT_INR`;
* denominator, `L_m = r_cb·(1+φ)·G_bad_m` → `CHARGEBACK_REALISATION_RATE`,
  `ANCILLARY_LOADING_PHI`.

`g`, `ℓ_m`, `r_cb` and `φ` enter multiplicatively, so moving them is a pure rescale of the
shipping `value_inr` / `loss_inr` and needs no regeneration. The low corner puts every numerator
primitive at the bottom of its `07-math.md` §5 range and every denominator primitive at the top;
the high corner reverses it. `asymmetry_range()` contains **no literal** — the endpoints are
whatever `COST_PRIMITIVE_RANGES` and the population produce.

On `validate` that is **2.5 – 530.3**, central **47.5**. Nine log-spaced points plus the central
value inserted so the cited operating point is a row rather than an interpolation.

At each point the asymmetry is realised by scaling `c_fp(m)` uniformly (`fp_cost_scale`). That
holds `L_m` fixed, so the sweep isolates the *asymmetry* instead of confounding it with the
absolute size of fraud loss.

## SURPRISE

### 1. `random` posts +0.69 savings. That is the loudest number in the ticket.

Under BMR a **uniform random score** scores +0.6929 on validate against `rules`' +0.6980 — a gap
of 0.005 — while ranking at PR-AUC 0.165, i.e. at prevalence. Across the whole sweep it stays
positive, and below asymmetry ≈ 9.5 it *beats* `rules`.

Nothing about any model produced that. The cost matrix did. When `c_fp` is small relative to
`L_m`, most merchants land on the correct side of their own threshold regardless of the score,
so the majority of the savings on this split is attributable to the decision layer's arithmetic
rather than to detection. This is `07-math.md` §6's AP-06 guard — *"the savings score is
manipulable via the cost matrix"* — arriving as a measurement instead of a warning.

**Consequence for T-0011 and the video: any margin quoted off the savings column must be quoted
against the `random` floor, not against zero.** A headline of the form "Rakshak saves X%" that
does not subtract random is not a claim about the model. This is now printed in
`results/summary.md` under its own heading rather than left for someone to notice.

### 2. Replacing the placeholder policy reversed the model ordering on savings

At T-0006b, under the top-K placeholder, `hmm` sat at −0.3625 and `rules` at +0.0038 — the HMM
below every baseline. Under BMR at the same central constants: `hmm` **+0.7464**, `rules`
+0.6980, `gbdt` +0.7392.

STATE.md predicted the *mechanism* before this policy existed: *"`budget_policy` is a top-K
placeholder, so a badly-calibrated-but-well-covering model is penalised twice — T-0007b's BMR
policy may change the ordering."* It did. The HMM's advantage is coverage (0.65 of bad merchants
flagged vs gbdt's 0.50) and its deficit is ranking and calibration; a rank-only policy charges
it for the deficit and pays it nothing for the coverage, while a cost-optimal policy that can
HOLD does the opposite.

**That is an explanation, not a vindication, and it must not be reported as one.** The HMM is
still worse on PR-AUC (0.4994 vs 0.6778) and much worse on Brier (0.3149 vs 0.1242), and item 1
above says most of the savings level is the cost matrix anyway. Nothing was tuned; the only
change is which policy converts scores into actions.

### 3. T-0007a's oracle-dominance invariant fired, and it was right to — on a category error

The first run of the sweep crashed at low asymmetry:

```
'trivial: hold-everything' scores +0.0000 > ceiling
'oracle (review knapsack, perfect foresight)' at -2.7421
```

**This is not a mis-specified cost matrix.** `review_knapsack_oracle` may only PASS and REVIEW,
so it is the best *review-only, ≤K* allocation and nothing more. Under a low false-positive
cost, holding is nearly free and averts nearly all loss, so hold-everything beats it — and so
does any policy allowed to hold, including T-0007b's. T-0007a asserted every policy against both
ceilings, which was sound only while the scored policy was `budget_policy` (PASS and REVIEW
only). T-0007b's whole job was to delete that policy.

T-0007a wrote this down before it bit, twice: `tests/test_cost.py`'s header says *"nothing forces
it above hold-everything"*, and its logbook's OPEN item 2 says *"if any model exceeds the
knapsack ceiling, that is a finding about `budget_policy`'s action class, not a licence to move a
constant."* Both were correct in advance.

The fix is `policy.assert_ceilings_dominate`, which scopes each ceiling to the action class it
actually bounds: the hindsight ceiling against **every** policy, the knapsack ceiling against the
**review-only** class (`pass-everything` is its one member in any run). **No constant was moved,
no sweep point was dropped, and the knapsack ceiling is still computed and printed at every
point** — including where it goes negative, in its own table:

| asymmetry | knapsack ceiling | hindsight ceiling | ≥ hold-everything |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.5 | −2.1503 | +0.1985 | **no** |
| 18.5 | −0.3777 | +0.6495 | **no** |
| 36.2 | +0.1500 | +0.7837 | yes |
| 530.3 | +0.6069 | +0.9000 | yes |

**The review-only ceiling stops being a ceiling at or below asymmetry 18.5 on this split.** That
boundary did not exist as a measured quantity before this ticket.

The uncomfortable corollary, and it should reach the video: `gap to knapsack oracle` in
`summary.md` is now negative for every model, because the models can hold and the ceiling cannot.
The column is kept and labelled a category error rather than deleted, because deleting it would
hide why. Read `gap to hindsight oracle`.

### 4. FR-020(d)'s threshold column is degenerate and would have shipped looking fine

The first draft reported the median PASS/HOLD threshold `p*` over all merchants. It read
**1.0000 at every asymmetry** — which would have gone into `sensitivity.md` as "the threshold
does not move with the cost matrix", the exact opposite of Elkan (2001)'s point.

The cause: 80 of the 100 validate merchants never transact in a bad state, so `L_m = 0` and
`p* = c_fp / c_fp = 1` by construction. The median is pinned by merchants for whom the threshold
is undefined in any useful sense. The at-risk median (`L_m > 0`) moves 0.0112 → 0.7085 across the
sweep. Both columns now ship, so the degenerate one cannot be quoted alone.
