## DID

### 1. Surveyed six datasets and verified every licence at source

The five the ticket names, plus **Online Retail II** (UCI 502). Every licence was read at the
source URL, not assumed. The full table is in `results/calibration_gap.md`; the load-bearing
findings:

- **BAF is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** — confirmed at the Kaggle dataset page, as the ticket suspected.
  There is a trap next to it: `github.com/feedzai/bank-account-fraud` ships an **Apache-2.0
  `LICENSE`, and that covers the code, not the data.** Anyone who checks the repo instead of
  the dataset page will record the wrong licence. NC + share-alike is fine for evaluation
  inside a git-ignored `data/`; it is **not vendorable** into an MIT repo.
- **ULB** is ODbL (database) + DbCL v1.0 (contents), from the Kaggle page's structured
  metadata. **PaySim** is CC BY-SA 4.0. **Sparkov** is CC0 — the most permissive of the six.
- **IEEE-CIS** is governed by competition rules requiring per-entrant acceptance. The rules
  page is behind the login wall and **could not be read at source**, so it is recorded as
  unverified rather than guessed.

### 2. Downloaded, hashed and manifested one dataset

**Online Retail II** — 45,622,418 bytes,
`sha256 572e36277c2390fbfde10664750731e0a86f55e33470d91919085f0408e67bfb`, retrieved
2026-08-29, CC BY 4.0, not subsampled. 1,067,371 line items normalised to **48,374 invoices**
in the canonical schema (`timestamp`, `amount`, `payer_id`, `is_refund`). A source row is a
line item; a row here is a payment, which is the unit the generator emits.

`data/external/online_retail_ii.manifest.json` is the provenance artifact the README cites.

### 3. Built the profiler and the gap diff

`build_profile(df) -> dict` measures eleven marginals on **one merchant's** stream. The same
function runs over the real data and over the generator's own output, so both columns of the
diff are computed identically rather than hand-transcribed — which also means the generator
column captures *emergent* values (`top_decile_payer_share` has no named constant; it falls out
of `_REPEAT_CONCENTRATION = 2.5`).

Per merchant, not pooled: pooling the generator's 500 merchants folds a 60–30,000 INR
cross-merchant AOV spread into `amount_log_sd` and makes it incomparable with one real
retailer's within-merchant dispersion. Generator side is **healthy merchants only**
(`typology == NONE`, 400 of 500) so no injected typology contaminates the baseline.

Twelve tests, all green, all against a 50-row committed fixture and never the network. Every
expected value is closed-form or hand-counted: the fixture's non-refund amounts are 15 each of
1.00 / 10.00 / 100.00, so `amount_log_sd` must be exactly `ln(10)·sqrt(2/3)`; daily counts are
5,5,5,5,10,10,10, so the Fano factor must be exactly 6/7. The SHA-256 expectation came from
`sha256sum`, not from `hashlib`.

### 4. The measured gap, and the T-0016 decision

Of the eight ratio-scale marginals, **five diverge by ≥1.9x and four by ≥5x; the widest is
32.5x** (`txns_per_active_day_mean`). `refund_rate` 7.81x, `daily_count_fano_factor` 16.0x,
`new_payer_frac` 0.18x, `amount_log_sd` 1.94x.

**Recommendation: the `divergence large` branch. T-0016 does not run.** The reasoning is in
SURPRISE below, and in `results/calibration_gap.md` under "The decision this file gates".

### 5. Two defects found on the way

- **`.gitignore`'s manifest exception did not work.** It read `data/*` then
  `!data/**/*.manifest.json`. Git does not descend into an excluded directory, so the negation
  could never match and the manifest the ticket calls "the provenance artifact the README cites"
  was silently unstageable. Fixed by re-including each level: `!data/external/`,
  `data/external/*`, `!data/external/*.manifest.json`. Verified with `git check-ignore -v`.
- **BAF cannot be fetched on this machine.** It is Kaggle-only (the Feedzai repo lists no other
  source) and there is no `~/.kaggle/kaggle.json`. `fetch()` raises rather than returning
  nothing, and the downloader keeps a credential path so T-0012 can run the moment a token
  exists. **T-0012 is blocked on a credential, not on code.**

## SURPRISE

**The licence gate — which the ticket called "a gate, not a footnote" and which I spent the
first hour on — rejected nothing.** Not one of the six candidates was dropped for its licence.
Two died on **access** (Kaggle credentials), two on **syntheticity**, one on **granularity**,
one on **structure**. The gate I was told to guard was not the gate that fired. I would have
found the real constraint faster by asking "can I download this, and is it real?" before
asking "may I use this?".

**Worse: the best schema match in the entire survey is a simulation.** Sparkov
(`kartik2112/fraud-detection`) has `merchant`, `category`, `amt`, `trans_date_trans_time` — it
is the only public dataset with a merchant axis *and* a category *and* timestamps, it is 1.85M
rows, and it is **CC0, the most permissive licence in the table.** It is also the output of a
generator whose amount and category profiles are as hand-chosen as ours. Calibrating our
generator on it would have produced a beautiful `calibration_gap.md` full of small divergences
that measured nothing but two authors' shared intuitions. The pull toward it was real: it was
the row that would have made this ticket look most successful. **A CC0 licence does not make a
simulation real, and a small divergence against a simulation is not evidence.**

**The unflattering finding the video needs.** One divergence is not a parameter being wrong.
`daily_count_fano_factor` is **1.0 for our generator by construction** — `rng.poisson(lam)` has
variance equal to its mean, always. The real stream reads **12.3**. Real merchants have bursty
days, promotions, weekly wholesale cycles; a Poisson merchant does not, at any rate. **No value
of any generator constant can close that marginal.** The daily-count process is the thing the
7-day emission windows are built on, so the emission features the HMM consumes are computed
over a volume process whose dispersion is off by more than an order of magnitude.

**And the reason T-0016 should still be cut, which is not the reason I expected.** I assumed
the argument would be "the gap is too large to close in the time available". The real argument
is that **the empirical side is n = 1 merchant** — a UK B2B gift-ware wholesaler trading in
GBP, closed on Saturdays, doing 80 invoices a day. Recalibrating a 500-merchant Indian-payments
generator toward that shop's marginals would replace a limitation we have now *measured* with
one nobody could characterise at all. The 32.5x on `txns_per_active_day_mean` is mostly the
statement "a wholesaler is busier than the median small merchant", not a defect. That is
precisely why the gap file states the caveats in the same breath as the numbers, and why I did
not mark the awkward rows "not comparable": suppressing a number because it flatters the
generator less is the same act as tuning it, and both were forbidden.

**One thing I did quietly wrong and then fixed.** The first render expressed
`hour_of_day_mean` as a ratio — "x0.82" for 12.75h against 15.60h. A clock hour is
interval-scale; it has no true zero, so that ratio is meaningless and it made a 2.85-hour shift
look like an 18% error. It now reports an absolute difference. Nobody would have caught it in
review, and it was in a table whose whole purpose is honest measurement.

## OPEN / NEEDS A DECISION

1. **The manifest is the one file under `data/` intended to be committed.** `git status
   --porcelain -uall -- data/` shows exactly one entry —
   `data/external/online_retail_ii.manifest.json` — and the 45 MB zip, the normalised parquet
   and all synthetic data are ignored. The ticket's build section requires the manifest
   (`data/external/*.manifest.json`, "the README cites it") while its `Done when` says "nothing
   under `data/` is committed". The orchestrator must resolve that literal contradiction. My
   read: the raw data must never be committed and is not; the manifest is the deliberate
   exception `.gitignore` has always encoded, and the README now cites its path.
2. **T-0012 is blocked on a Kaggle credential, not on code.** BAF is Kaggle-only and no token
   exists on this machine. `rakshak.data.download --dataset baf` will fetch, hash and manifest
   it the moment `~/.kaggle/kaggle.json` is present. Until then, `README.md:25-28` and
   `CLAUDE.md` still assert *"The decision layer is additionally validated on BAF (Feedzai,
   NeurIPS 2022), a public benchmark derived from real bank data"* — a claim the repo cannot
   back. T-0017 already recorded the fallback: strike the sentence, do not soften it.
3. **BAF's granularity is worse than the board assumes.** It is bank **account-opening
   applications** — no amount, no timestamp, no payer id, no merchant. It can validate the
   decision layer on a real-derived imbalanced tabular problem, which is what T-0012 wants, but
   it cannot inform a single one of this profile's marginals. Anyone reading "T-0015 procures
   BAF for calibration" has the wrong model of what BAF is.
4. **`daily_count_fano_factor` deserves a line in the video.** It is the cleanest example in the
   repo of a limitation that is structural rather than tunable, and it was found by measurement
   rather than admitted in prose.
5. **`openpyxl>=3.1` was added to `pyproject.toml`** because UCI ships Online Retail II as
   `.xlsx` only. MIT — compatible with the permissive-licence-only constraint. It is used by the
   one-off download path, never by `make eval`.
6. **The empirical profile rests on one merchant.** If anyone later wants a defensible
   recalibration target, the missing ingredient is *many real merchants*, and no public source
   supplies it. That is the same wall as `06-requirements.md:28`, arrived at from the marginals
   side rather than the labels side.
