## Built

| Feature | Family | Buckets | Divergence | Declared |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `t_wasserstein_7d` | F2 | 32 log-spaced amount bins | 1-Wasserstein on binned CDFs | 1024 B |
| `i_mix_jsd` | F3 | 7 instruments | Jensen-Shannon | 232 B |
| `f_decline_entropy` | F5 | 8 hashed decline codes | Shannon entropy (no baseline) | 224 B |
| `h_hourly_jsd` | F7 | 24 hours | Jensen-Shannon | 768 B |

All four are one class: a frozen baseline histogram, a trailing-7-day ring of completed
daily histograms, and today's incomplete histogram kept out of the ring so `value()` can be
correct on an empty day without mutating anything. A subclass declares its bucket function
in both languages and its divergence, and gets both runners.

**The divergence functions are shared between the runners literally, not by convention.**
`jensen_shannon`, `wasserstein_binned` and `shannon_entropy` take an (M × bins) matrix; the
online path calls them with M=1 and the offline path with M=merchants. There is no scalar
transliteration to drift out of sync, which is why the T2 parity differences are at machine
epsilon rather than merely under 1e-9.

`t_wasserstein_7d` is computed in **log-rupee space**, on the same 32-bin log histogram
T-120's `t_p95_median_ratio` already maintains. That is the right space twice: ticket sizes
are lognormal so the bins are roughly equal-mass, and the distance is then in decades rather
than rupees, which makes it comparable across a kirana and a jeweller. A Wasserstein in
rupees would have been a GMV feature wearing a distribution feature's name — precisely the
absolute-level mistake register rule 1 exists to prevent.

Declared T1 1720 B + T2 2248 B = **3968 B against NFR-04's 4096**, with 128 B of headroom.

## Surprised

**An empty trailing window is not free zero divergence, and the naive version turns every
dormant merchant into a permanent mix-drift alert.** JSD between an empty distribution and
a concentrated baseline is exactly **0.5** — not 0 — because the mixture halves the
baseline's mass and the baseline's own KL term survives. Left alone, `i_mix_jsd` and
`h_hourly_jsd` would have parked every merchant that stopped trading at a constant 0.5,
which is a mix-drift feature reporting dormancy: a signal `v_dormant_burst` already carries
and carries better. It would also have passed parity perfectly, because both runners
compute the same wrong thing.

Caught by a test written to assert the *opposite* — that the empty case reads 0.0 — which
failed and turned out to be right about the intent and wrong about the arithmetic. Fixed
with a `comparable()` mask applied identically on both runners, and the test now pins the
0.5 as the trap so the mask cannot be deleted as dead code later.

**Real generator output moved the worst parity difference by four orders of magnitude, and
it was a T1 feature that moved it.** The whole registry — 28 features, both runners — over
real generator output at 60 merchants × 45 days: **max diff 1.871e-11**, in
`h_interarrival_cv`. On `synthetic_stream` the same registry sits at 3.553e-15. The cause
is float64 associativity: inter-arrival gaps are nanosecond quantities summed in different
orders by the two runners, and the CV then runs them through `sumsq/n − mean²`, which is
catastrophically cancelling. It is still 50× inside NFR-08's tolerance, but it is the one
feature in the registry whose margin is not enormous, and if a future feature needs a
tighter tolerance this is the one that breaks first. Recorded rather than fixed, because
the fix is a Welford CV and that is a bigger change than the margin currently justifies.

**`synthetic_stream` cannot test a mix feature at all.** It emits one instrument, one
decline code, one device hash and one MCC group, so `i_mix_jsd` and `f_decline_entropy` see
a single bucket in it. Their parity there is real and their *range* is entirely untested.
This is the sharpest form of T-120's dependency deviation: parity is a statement about two
runners agreeing, and it is completely silent on whether they agree about anything. The
last two tests in `test_tier2_parity.py` close it — one replays real generator output
through every registered feature, and the other asserts that stream actually contains ≥4
instruments, ≥2 decline codes and ≥12 distinct hours, so the first cannot go green by
being handed an empty bucket.

## Cut, with reasons

**`d_refund_latency_med` — cut permanently. Not online-computable in bounded state.**
The latency of a refund is `refund.event_time − original_capture.event_time`, and the
original is reachable only through `refund_of`. Computing it online means retaining the
timestamp of every capture that might still be refunded, and the set of open captures has
no bound: a refund can arrive weeks later, and a merchant with 500 captures a day would
carry tens of thousands of open ids. There is no sketch that fixes this — a sketch could
estimate a distribution of latencies, but it cannot answer "when was event X captured".

This is **register rule 2 doing exactly the job the register says it is there to do**:
"Online-computable or out. This kills several attractive features and that is the point."
It is the first feature this sprint that the rule actually killed, and it deserves a line
in `LIMITATIONS.md`. It is also worth noting *why* it is attractive — instant-refund
laundering (R5) is a real typology and this is the feature aimed at it — and what a real
deployment would do instead: emit the latency as a field on the refund event at write time,
in the generator and in the payment system, so the feature becomes a plain daily aggregate.
That is a `schemas.py` change and it is the right one.

**`i_bin_hhi` — cut for budget.** The register's own instruction: "If it goes over, cut
`i_bin_hhi` (512 B) first — it is the most redundant with `g_new_payer_ratio`." Followed.

**`g_payer_hhi` and `g_device_reuse_rate` — cut for budget.** A 28-day payer Herfindahl
needs the window resident, and after T1's honest 1720 B and the four features above there
are 128 B left. Both were designed before being cut, and the designs are worth keeping: a
**fixed hashed-bucket sketch** (hash the payer id into 64 buckets, HHI over bucket shares)
rather than the register's top-k counter, because a top-k counter has an eviction policy
and an eviction policy has to be replicated exactly by the offline runner to keep parity —
which is a Python replay of the online algorithm, not an offline runner. A hashed bucket
has no eviction policy and is a one-line `group_by` offline. Same argument replaces the
register's HyperLogLog for `g_unique_payers_z` / `g_new_payer_ratio` with **linear
counting** over a 256-bit bitmap: deterministic, exactly parity-able, 32 B, and adequate at
these cardinalities. Whoever picks these up should start there.

**The root cause of all three budget cuts is the one T-120 already reported**: six T1
features keep private rings over what is largely the same daily series. One shared
per-merchant daily ring on `MerchantState` would return roughly 1 KB, which is `i_bin_hhi`
plus `g_payer_hhi` plus room to spare. It needs a slot in the frozen `state.py`.

## Broke

- `registry.REGISTRY.values()` cannot be reconstructed uniformly: `Tier1Spec` and
  `HistogramSpec` take `warmup_days`, `StaticSpec` takes nothing. The whole-registry test
  now branches on the base class. A single `__init__(**config)` on `FeatureSpec` would be
  cleaner and is a frozen-file change.
- `polars` has no `crc32`, so `f_decline_entropy`'s offline bucket goes through
  `map_elements`. It is slow and it is correct; a codebook join would be faster and needs a
  vocabulary that does not exist until the generator's decline codes are frozen.
