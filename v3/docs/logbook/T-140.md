## Built

- **`src/rakshak/models/dataset.py`** — the step nobody had built. Lane B produced a feature
  layer and Lane C a harness, and there was no `(merchant-day × feature)` matrix between
  them; `make features` called a `rakshak.cli features` subcommand that did not exist and
  `data/` was empty. It replays the event stream through the **online** runner (`update`),
  merchant by merchant, and reads `value()` at every epoch. `last_day` defaults to the last
  validation day and *refuses* a value inside the test span, so the test rows do not exist
  rather than being skipped.
- **`src/rakshak/models/rung0_floors.py`** — the four floors as scorers, so they can go
  through the identical decision layer and metric suite as the rungs. Same construction
  `savings_of_ranking` uses (top-K by score per day → REVIEW), so a Rung-0 row's `savings`
  and the matching `savings_floor_*` column on a Rung-2 row are the same arithmetic on the
  same rows.
- **`src/rakshak/models/rung1_rules.py`** — twelve graded rules over the register. Fixed
  thresholds, declared in the file, not fitted. Each rule contributes
  `clip((value − threshold) / scale, 0, 1)` rather than a bit, because a binary twelve-rule
  engine emits about eight distinct scores and the top-K selection degenerates into a
  tiebreak on row order.
- **`src/rakshak/cli.py`** — `features`, `train` and `eval` subcommands, plus `_guard`. See
  the carry-forward section below; it is the more important half of this ticket.
- **`tests/unit/test_rungs_0_1.py`** (13 cases) and **`tests/unit/test_cli_guard.py`** (15).

## The carry-forward: the guard on the one-way door is wired

`cli._guard(split)` calls **`require_unlocked_or_refuse(split)` then `verify_lock(ROOT)`**,
in that order, before a single row is read, on **both** `train` and `eval`. The split guard
runs first deliberately: a run that is not permitted at all should not first spend time
telling you the generator hash moved.

Asserted in `tests/unit/test_cli_guard.py`:
- only the literal `"1"` unlocks; `"true"`, `"TRUE"`, `"True"`, `"yes"`, `"y"`, `"0"`, `""`,
  `"1 "`, `" 1"` and the variable being absent all refuse;
- `eval --split test` pointed at an **empty directory** raises `SplitLockedError`, not
  `FileNotFoundError` — which is how the *ordering* gets asserted rather than assumed;
- `train` and `eval --split val` both hard-fail with `LockMismatchError` when `ROOT` is
  monkeypatched to a tree whose `src/rakshak/eval/` is absent;
- the real lock verifies clean on `eval_module_sha256` and `open_count` is 0.

## Surprised

**`volume_rank` is not a dumb floor on this generator.** It scores **+0.16999** savings and
beats every rung including Rung 3 (0.10112). Every typology raises transaction intensity —
`intensity_multiple` runs 1.5× to 22× — and `true_loss_amount_inr` scales with volume, so by
the validation window the fraud merchants *are* among the largest merchants and "alert on the
nine biggest" is a partially-informed detector. Its week-over-week alert Jaccard is exactly
1.000: it names the same nine merchants for thirty days. **Every rung is FLOOR-FAIL against
it**, which means charter adoption gate D.2 fails for all of them. `LIMITATIONS.md` §8.3.

**The merchant-day unit double-charges a merchant-level loss, and it inflates `all_hold` into
looking profitable.** `row_cost` charges `loss` on every merchant-day a fraud is left to
PASS, and `Truth.loss_inr` is a per-merchant total — so a merchant that turns on day 40 of 180
is billed 140 times its own loss. On the first run `all_hold` came back at **+0.685 savings**
and would have FLOOR-FAILed every rung for an accounting reason. `cli._build_truth` now
amortises the loss over the days from onset to the end of the horizon; `all_hold` becomes
−28.128, which is the role that floor is meant to play. The decision is in `cli.py`, not in
the frozen `eval/`, and it is named in `LIMITATIONS.md` §8.9 because it moves every savings
number in the project.

**Rung 1 out-ranks LightGBM by 5× on PR-AUC** (0.33644 against 0.06570) and loses on every
operational metric that follows the ranking. Detail in T-141's logbook and §8.4.

**Three registered features are effectively constant on real data** — `f_retry_burst_rate`
non-zero on 584 of 852,930 rows, `t_micro_share` on 1,030, `t_new_max_event` on 1,651. Parity
is green on all three; they simply almost never fire. Two of Rung 1's twelve rules are
therefore close to dead weight. §8.8.

## Broke

**The `--seed` option was decorative on `train` and I nearly shipped it.** The first
five-seed sweep returned *byte-identical* PR-AUC at every seed, which is what made it
visible: `cli.train` was calling `rung2_lgbm.train(...)` with `DEFAULT_PARAMS` and using the
CLI seed only for the model filename. LightGBM reads four separate seeds (`seed`,
`bagging_seed`, `feature_fraction_seed`, `data_random_seed`) and all four stayed at 42.
Fixed with `DEFAULT_PARAMS.with_seed(seed)`, and the whole sweep re-run. Worth naming as a
pattern: **a seed sweep whose results do not move is not a stable model, it is an unwired
seed**, and identical-to-six-decimals is the tell.

The materialisation is **51.7 minutes** of single-core Python for 12.93M events across 10,000
merchants × 150 epochs — five times what the 300-merchant smoke run extrapolated to, most of
it because the smoke extrapolation was measured while nothing else was competing for the
core. Cached to `data/v2/features.parquet` (68 MB, 852,930 rows × 49 columns) so it is paid
once.
