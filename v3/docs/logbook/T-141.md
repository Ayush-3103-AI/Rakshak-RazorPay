## Built

- **`src/rakshak/models/rung2_lgbm.py`** — `HParams`, `TrainedRung`, `train`,
  `explain_column`. Reason codes from native `pred_contrib=True`, top 3 by absolute
  contribution, rendered through the registry's `human_template`. No SHAP dependency.
- Nine unit cases in **`tests/unit/test_rung2.py`**.

Three decisions are each a decision *not* to do something, and all three are in the module
docstring:

- **No post-hoc calibrator.** LightGBM's binary objective is logloss, a proper scoring rule,
  so the raw sigmoid is already a probability estimate. A held-out calibration slice on this
  dataset would hold **one or two** positive merchants, and an isotonic fit on two positives
  is a step function with a random step. Measured ECE on the raw probability is 0.00988,
  against a base rate of 0.996% — the mean prediction tracks the base rate.
- **No `scale_pos_weight`, no `is_unbalance`.** Both lift ranking metrics a little and
  destroy calibration completely, and `capacity.expected_costs` consumes the score *as a
  probability*. An uncalibrated score makes every rupee figure downstream arithmetic on a
  meaningless quantity.
- **No early stopping on the validation split.** Early stopping is model selection; doing it
  against the split the result is reported on makes the number optimistic by an unknown
  amount. Tree count fixed at 300, `num_leaves` 15, `min_child_samples` 200, `lambda_l2` 10.

## Surprised

**Eight labelled positive merchants. That is the whole story.** `available_labels(day 119)`
returns **12** positive merchants platform-wide; **8** are in the train fold; they become 960
positive rows of 801,240. The generator emits 147 fraud merchants, 81 merchants are censored
and 60 of those are fraud. The arithmetic is the spec's: onset uniform on days 20–165, plus
Exponential(21) to the dispute, plus Uniform(45, 120) to availability — so a merchant is
trainable at day 119 only if onset + 66…141 ≤ 119.

This is FR-020 and 10-eval-harness-spec §1 working exactly as written, and it is the binding
constraint on every Lane D result. **Compute was never the constraint: 7.6 s against a
20-minute budget, 0.49 MB against 20 MB. Labels were.** `LIMITATIONS.md` §8.1.

**The static rule engine beats the incumbent on PR-AUC by 5.1×** — 0.33644 against 0.06570.
The charter says the bar is LightGBM and not the rule engine, and on the ranking metric this
measurement does not agree. It is not a reversal: Rung 1 loses on precision@K (0.064 vs
0.186), recall@K (0.029 vs 0.090), day-30 detection (0.000 vs 0.118), alert stability (0.203
vs 0.643) and calibration (0.0163 vs 0.0099). A rule engine that ranks well and cannot fill
an analyst queue is not the better system. But the divergence between PR-AUC and everything
measured under capacity K is itself the finding, and it is exactly why FR-021 puts them on
one row. §8.4.

**Nothing detects anything quickly.** d7 and d14 are **0.000 for every rung and every
floor**, and median TTD is `inf` across the board — more than half the scoreable positives
are never alerted on. Charter §2 makes TTD an equal-standing win condition; on this
measurement it does not discriminate between rungs at all. §8.7.

## Broke

`p_declared_monthly_gmv` is used as the decision layer's `exposure_inr`. The first attempt
used *daily* declared volume (`/30`), and at 1% prevalence
`benefit = 0.8·p·exposure − review_cost` was negative for essentially every row, so the
cost-aware selector emitted **zero alerts** and savings collapsed to exactly `all_pass`.
Monthly declared volume — "the standing exposure this merchant represents" — fills capacity
and is the reading that matches the cost matrix's horizon. Feature-derived and point-in-time;
`true_loss_amount_inr` is never in the decision path, which is what keeps the oracle above
every rung.

## Cut, deliberately

Nothing. The tuning that would obviously have helped — a lead-time window on the positive
labels, `scale_pos_weight`, early stopping on val — was not done, and each is named above
with the reason.
