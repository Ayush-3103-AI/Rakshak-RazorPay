## Measured (10,000 merchants)
| clause | target | measured |
|---|---|---|
| all four states produced | 4/4 | 4/4 |
| `label_available_at > label_event_at >= drift_onset_at` | 0 violations | 0, over 90 hypothesis examples |
| unreported rate | 0.15 | within 4 binomial sd |
| spurious chargeback rate | 0.003 | within 4 binomial sd |
| dispute delay | [45, 120] days | min 45.0, max 120.0 |

At the shipped scenario, roughly 56% of fraud merchants resolve inside the 180-day
horizon; the rest are censored. That is the delayed-label reality the ticket exists to
create, and it will make the effective training prevalence lower than 1.47%.

## Surprised
**The exponential draw is allowed to return exactly zero and that is correct, not a bug.**
The invariant is `label_event_at >= drift_onset_at` — a dispute filed the instant the
drift began is legal; one filed before it is not. It reads like an off-by-one waiting to
happen and it is the reason the property test sweeps `fraud_to_dispute_mean_days` down to
0.5 days.

**The genuinely non-obvious modelling point is that an unreported positive is a *wrong*
label, not a missing one.** `label = 0`, `is_censored = false`, on a merchant that really
did defraud the platform. Conflating it with censoring would delete exactly the weak
supervision the harness is built to expose, and it would do so while every count still
looked plausible. The test asserts the two sets are disjoint.

**Constructing every emitted row through `schemas.Label` was cheaper than restating its
invariants and is strictly stronger** — the contract cannot drift away from the generator
without that property test failing.

## Broke
Nothing. 8/8 on the first run, and the hypothesis suites found no counterexample across
the degenerate corners (all fraud, nothing reported, a half-day dispute delay).
