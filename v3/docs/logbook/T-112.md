## Surprised
**Three, and all three changed a number in the config.**

1. **At Fano 12.25 the shapeless persona's best week is already 2.6x its own mean.** The
   first L2 test compared raw daily peak-to-median and *failed backwards*: L1 scored 18.0
   and L2 scored 13.0, because a raw daily peak measures overdispersion, not a sale. Even
   after smoothing to a 7-day mean, a genuine 4.5x sale window only reaches 3.6x against
   L1's 2.6x. That gap is the finding, not a weak test — a legitimate spike is genuinely
   hard to separate from arrival noise, which is precisely why L2 is in the population.

2. **L5's low inter-arrival CV cannot survive a cross-day measurement, and chasing it
   would have broken FR-003.** Gaps within a day are constant by construction, but the
   spacing is `86400/n_d` and `n_d` is overdispersed, so cross-day gaps inherit the count
   dispersion and the CV lands near 0.48. The tempting fix — a per-persona Fano override
   for L5 — would have pulled the population Fano off 12.25 and broken the gate that
   justifies the whole arrival rewrite. Measuring *within* a day instead is both cheaper
   and more honest: the midnight gap is an artefact of daily aggregation, not a property
   of the arrival rhythm. Written up in the test docstring so the choice is visible.

3. **The L3-vs-R1 separation needed a cohort mean and onset alignment, and R1's convexity
   had to go from 3.2 to 5.0.** Per-merchant R² is pure noise at this dispersion. Averaging
   R1 merchants on *calendar* days smears the convexity into something that looks linear
   for a reason that has nothing to do with the generator; aligning on each merchant's own
   `drift_onset_at` is the only measurement that means anything.

Also worth recording: the first engine draft inflated total volume by ~50% through a
confounder bug (see T-114) and the full run came out at 21M transactions before the fix.
