## Built

`src/rakshak/features/cohort.py`:

- `assign_cohorts` — `(mcc_group, gmv_decile, vintage_bucket)` with the FR-013 backoff
  chain: full key → `mcc_group` → global, taking the first level with ≥ 30 members. Deciles
  are cut on declared GMV, which is an onboarding fact, so a merchant's cohort is fixed at
  approval and cannot drift with the behaviour the layer exists to measure. `CohortLevel`
  is kept per merchant rather than discarded, because it is the diagnostic that explains a
  residual which did nothing.
- `loo_median` — leave-one-out median by **rank arithmetic on one sorted array**, not N
  recomputes. Removing sorted position `r` leaves an array whose reduced index `j` maps
  back to `j` when `j < r` and `j+1` otherwise; both median positions are closed-form in
  the parity of `n-1`, so each member's excluding-self median is two gathers off one sort.
  O(n log n) per cohort per epoch. Cross-checked against the O(N²) definition on eleven
  sizes covering both parities of `n` and of `n-1`, plus a tie-heavy case, because a quiet
  day's cohort is mostly zeros and a value-keyed implementation would exclude the wrong
  merchant.
- `residuals` / `residual_matrix` — the per-feature and per-epoch forms, cross-checked
  against each other. A merchant absent from an epoch's values is **dropped from its
  cohort, not defaulted to 0.0**: stuffing it in at zero drags the median toward zero on
  exactly the days a confounder is lifting everyone.
- `eb_shrink` / `shrunk_z` — the FR-011 cold-start half. `(n·sample + k·prior)/(n+k)` with
  `k = 30`, so the crossover is one warmup window, which is where the baseline freezes
  anyway. **Both moments shrink, not only the mean**: shrinking the centre while keeping a
  cold merchant's near-zero observed sd turns the shrinkage's own residual error into a
  100σ alert, which is the cold-start fix becoming the cold-start bug.

17 T1 features carry `has_cohort_residual`. Residual parity across all 17 over a 40-day
stream: **max diff 5.329e-15**.

## Surprised

### The ticket's `Done when:` clause is arithmetically unsatisfiable

> "with prevalence=0 under confounder P2, mean |residual| < 0.25 while mean |raw z| > 1.0"

Measured on `f_auth_fail_rate_z` — the feature the register itself nominates for this test —
at prevalence=0 with confounders on, at both 3,000 and 10,000 merchants, on the P2 outage
day (simulation day 97, 2026-04-08):

| | mean \|raw z\| | mean \|residual\| | median z | median residual | alert rate @ \|·\|>3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P2 outage day** | 3.301 | 3.117 | +1.790 | +0.005 | 0.390 → 0.231 |
| **null day (96)** | 0.691 | 0.558 | −0.378 | −0.000 | 0.024 → 0.028 |

The first half of the clause passes comfortably. The second half misses by 12×.

**And the null day is the proof that it is not an implementation failure.** On a day with
*no confounder at all*, mean |residual| is already **0.558**. A z-score has unit scale by
construction; E|N(0,1)| = 0.798. Subtracting a cohort median from a unit-scale quantity
cannot bring its mean absolute value below roughly 0.6 unless the within-cohort z-scores
agree to ±0.3σ, which no per-merchant z-score ever does. **A perfect residual layer and a
useless one both fail this clause.** The number 0.25 appears to have been written for the
*shift* in the residual, not for its absolute level.

The assertion is in the suite, worded exactly as the ticket words it, marked
`xfail(strict=True)` with the whole argument and the measured numbers in its `reason`.
It was not weakened. **The clause in `BOARD.md` needs rewriting to a common-mode or
alert-rate criterion before T-142 is judged against it**, and the proposal is at the bottom
of this entry.

### P2 is not a common-mode event in this feature, and that is the real K-1 finding

The layer does exactly what it was designed to do: P2 lifts the cohort median z to **+1.79**
and the median residual is **+0.005** — the shared component is removed to floating point.
What it cannot remove is the part that was never shared. Under P2 the raw z's standard
deviation goes from 2.00 on a null day to **5.58**, so the outage does not shift the
population, it *fans it out*. Lane A's own `confounders.py` says why, in its docstring:
every magnitude is converted per merchant as `1 + magnitude_sigma · sqrt(target_fano/λ)`,
times a persona sensitivity. A six-hour outage therefore hits a five-transaction-a-day
merchant several times harder than a five-hundred one, and a median can only subtract what
is common.

The consequence, measured: the residual cuts the confounder-driven alert rate from
**39.0% to 23.1%** of the population — a real 41% relative reduction, and still a gate-G5
catastrophe against a 2.4% null-day baseline. **The cohort residual is a partial defence
against platform drift, not the clean separation the charter hypothesised.** That is a
finding worth reporting either way and it arrives before T-142 rather than after.

The obvious lever is the one the cohort key already contains: `gmv_decile` correlates with
λ, which is what drives the heterogeneity, so a full-key cohort should residualise P2 far
better than an `mcc_group` one. It does not get the chance. **At 3,000 merchants the
backoff chain never reaches the full key at all** (0 full / 3,000 mcc_group), and even at
the charter's 10,000 only 81% do (8,126 full / 1,874 mcc_group / 0 global) — and the result
barely moves: mean |residual| 3.135 at 10,000 against 3.117 at 3,000. So the backoff is not
the binding constraint either. Reported, not patched.

### Everything above was invisible until the generator was real

The T-112 dependency the board named turned out to be load-bearing for a reason nobody
wrote down. **The transaction stream starts at simulation day 0, while merchants were
onboarded up to 120 days earlier** (`onboarding_spread_days: 120`). T-120 anchored every
feature's warmup window to `onboarded_at`, so for most of the population that window
contained no transactions at all: the baseline was `mean = 0, std = 0`, and every z divided
by the 1e-9 floor and clipped at 50. Against `synthetic_stream` — where every merchant is
onboarded on day 0 — this was invisible, and all 24 features were green.

Fixed by re-anchoring the warmup window to **the merchant's first observed event date**,
which both runners compute identically (`min(event_date)` offline, first `update` online)
and which never moves once set. It is also the better definition: it is the merchant's
first `warmup_days` of *visibility*, which is what a real deployment has. Parity re-run and
still clean at 3.553e-15.

The lesson for the board: "build against `synthetic_stream` and re-run against the
generator later" was the right call for *parity*, and would have been the wrong call if it
had ended there. Parity is a statement about two runners agreeing; it is silent about
whether they agree on something meaningful.

## Broke

- The first self-exclusion test was tautological. `[0,0,0,0,0,10]` has a median of 0 whether
  or not the 10 is included, because a median is robust — which is the point of using one.
  Rewritten to the cases where inclusion actually bites: a two-member cohort and a
  four-member cohort with half of it drifting, where including self halves the signal.
- `mypy --strict` rejected `dict[str, tuple[...]].setdefault(...).append(...)` and two
  numpy fancy-index returns typed as `Any`. Both real, both fixed rather than ignored.

## Proposed replacement for the `Done when:` clause

Stated so T-142 has something falsifiable to be judged against. Every number below is the
measured value at 3,000 merchants, so the thresholds are guards and not goals:

1. **Common mode removed.** Under P2 at prevalence=0, `|median residual| < 0.05` while
   `median raw z > 1.0`. *(measured: 0.005 vs +1.790)*
2. **Confounder-driven alerts materially reduced.** Alert rate at `|·| > 3` under P2 falls
   by at least 15% relative against the raw z. *(measured: 39.0% → 23.1%, a 41% reduction)*
3. **Not overclaimed.** The residual alert rate under P2 remains well above the null-day
   rate, and that gap is reported in `LIMITATIONS.md` as the unsolved part of K-4.
   *(measured: 23.1% vs 2.8%)*

All three are asserted in `tests/unit/test_cohort.py::test_what_the_cohort_residual_actually_does_under_p2`.
