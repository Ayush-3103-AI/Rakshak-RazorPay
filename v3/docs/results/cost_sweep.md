# Cost-asymmetry sweep — cycle 4, validation split

> Generated by `scripts/cost_sweep.py`. Do not hand-edit.

Every savings number published by this project before today was a **single point estimate** at one cost matrix. `metrics.CostParams`' own docstring says that is not enough: v1 measured the asymmetry at 47.5 / 13.1 / 61,368 against a literature band of 400–600, three orders of magnitude of spread. `sweep_cost_asymmetry` was built and unit-tested to answer this and had never been run on the ladder.

## 1. What was varied

- Swept: `false_hold_cost / mean_fraud_loss` over `ASYMMETRY_RATIOS` = [0.01, 0.1, 1.0, 10.0, 100.0], the five points declared in `10-eval-harness-spec.md` §2 and **not extended** — a denser grid chosen after seeing the curve is an argument, not a measurement.
- Held: `review_cost_inr` = 250, `p_catch` = 0.8, `fraud_loss_multiplier` = 1.0.
- Rows: validation split, K = 30, 6,025 merchants, 361,500 scored merchant-days, 3,865 positive rows, seeds [42, 43, 44, 45, 46], mean over seeds.
- Nothing was refitted. These are the decisions the committed models already make, re-priced.

**Where the shipped cost matrix sits on this grid.** The sweep's denominator is the mean `true_loss_amount_inr` over the fraud rows in this window, ₹51,954. Against it, the config's `false_hold_cost_inr` of ₹8,000 is a ratio of **0.15398** — inside. So every savings number this project has published sits within the range declared in `10-eval-harness-spec.md` §2, on the 'a wrong HOLD is cheap relative to a fraud' side. That is a property of this generator's loss distribution, not a tuning choice, and the grid is left as declared: extending it after seeing the tables is how a sweep becomes an argument. It does bound the reading — the tables below say the ranking is stable across the declared range, and the operating point is at or beyond the cheap-HOLD end of it, where the rungs do best.

One residual impurity, stated rather than hidden: `select_actions` ranks by `cost_pass - min(cost_review, cost_hold)`, so `cost_hold` still influences *which* rows are selected even when HOLD cannot be *taken*. Removing that would mean editing the locked selector. The effect is bounded — at low ratios `cost_hold` collapses toward `review_cost`, at high ratios `cost_review` is the minimum — but Table C is "HOLD unreachable", not "HOLD absent from the arithmetic".

## 5. What the sweep settles

**1. The savings result is stable across the whole declared range.** The best arm-B rung beats the `volume_rank` floor (+0.5240) at **5 of 5** swept ratios (0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100). A result that survives four orders of magnitude of cost asymmetry is a materially stronger claim than a win at one guessed ratio, and it is the claim a business case actually needs. Per-rung range under arm B:

   - `rung1`: +0.5227 to +0.5232 (spread 0.0005)
   - `rung2`: +0.5385 to +0.5388 (spread 0.0003)
   - `rung3`: +0.4947 to +0.4958 (spread 0.0010)
   - `rung4`: +0.5853 to +0.6001 (spread 0.0148) <- best at every ratio
   - `rung9`: +0.4788 to +0.4921 (spread 0.0133)

**2. The rungs do not out-rank the size floor on rupees. The decision layer is what puts them ahead.** Priced as pure rankings under the floors' own REVIEW-only convention (Table B), the best rung (`rung1`) loses to `volume_rank` by **0.2652**. This is LIMITATIONS.md §8.3a's mechanism restated at the level of the cost matrix: `volume_rank` is an exposure estimator, and on rupees an exposure estimator beats a fraud-probability ranker that is never told what is at stake.

**3. The margin survives the pricing asymmetry §4.3 disclosed, but not by much, and the decomposition should be reported rather than the headline alone.** Take `rung4`, the best row in Table A, at ratio 0.01:

   - Table A, as scored (HOLD permitted): **+0.5980**, a margin of **+0.0740** over the floor.
   - Table C, HOLD unreachable, everything else identical: **+0.5644**, a margin of **+0.0403**. It still beats the floor, at **5 of 5** ratios.
   - Table B, the raw ranking: **+0.2349**, a margin of **-0.2892** — negative, and by a wide margin.

So the HOLD privilege is worth about **45%** of the rung's margin over the floor, the exposure-aware re-ranking supplies the rest, and the score ranking on its own is worth less than nothing against a size ranking. §4.3 of the pre-registration disclosed the 33x asymmetry and declined to fix it inside a locked harness; this quantifies what it was worth. **The direction matters: the rung would still beat the floor with HOLD switched off**, so the FLOOR-FAIL verdict does not rest on the unfair half of the comparison — but a report that quotes +0.0740 without +0.0403 beside it is quoting the flattering one.

**4. What this does not settle.** Every number here is on the **validation** split (`open_count` is 0). None of it is a test-split result and none of it was pre-registered: the sweep ran after the ladder was scored, which makes it a robustness check on a measured result, not a gate, and it must not be reported as one. The latency half of charter §2 is untouched — savings is a rupee metric and says nothing about detection delay, and `rung4`, the best row in Table A, has `ttd_median_days` of `inf` in both arms.
